# Commutative (abelian): the order of the two operands does not matter

**Level:** 101 · one row of the table on [the laws of an operation](../../README.md), on its own page

**One line:** Commutativity is the one law that is independent of the other three, so it names a flavour rather than a level: a commutative semigroup, monoid or group is one where a · b = b · a, and the examples that fail it, strings, permutations and matrices, are the ones where order is the whole point.

## Laws required

any of the three, plus commutative, of the four on [the parent page](../../README.md#the-names-are-only-combinations).

## What it is, and the examples

An operation is **commutative** when a · b = b · a for all a and b. Unlike the other three laws, which stack (every group is a monoid, every monoid a semigroup), commutativity is independent of them: there are commutative semigroups that are not monoids (positive integers under +), non-commutative monoids (strings under concatenation), and non-commutative groups (the permutations of three objects, and invertible matrices). A commutative group is called **abelian**, after Niels Henrik Abel, and its operation is usually written +.

The program sorts the examples. Commutative: ℤ under + and ×, max, gcd, ∪ and ∩. Not commutative: string concatenation ("ab" ≠ "ba"), composition of permutations (the program finds the pair), and 2 × 2 matrices, where the order of two rotations in space, or of two moves of a Rubik's cube, matters.

Why a separate word: with commutativity the operation table is symmetric about its diagonal, sums can be reordered freely, and ℤ, ℚ, ℝ and ℂ are all abelian under +, which is why the laws of an operation page says the news in a new list is usually the law that is missing. The first structure most people meet where it is missing is matrix multiplication, in [linear algebra](../../../../reading_guides/linear_algebra/README.md).

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Przemienność, a · b = b · a, jest niezależna od pozostałych trzech praw, więc nazywa odmianę, a nie poziom: półgrupa, monoid lub grupa mogą być przemienne albo nie. Grupa przemienna to grupa abelowa, od Nielsa Abela, z działaniem pisanym zwykle jako +. Przemienne: ℤ z + i ×, max, gcd, ∪, ∩. Nieprzemienne: sklejanie napisów, składanie permutacji, mnożenie macierzy; tam kolejność jest całą treścią.
