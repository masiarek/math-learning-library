# The midpoint formula

**Level:** 101 · for anyone starting precalculus

**One line:** The midpoint of a segment is the average of the two x's and the average of the two y's; "halfway" is two claims, equally far from both ends *and* on the segment, and the first alone describes a whole line of points.

## The idea

The segment from P₁ = (x₁, y₁) to P₂ = (x₂, y₂) has one point exactly halfway along it, the **midpoint** M:

> **M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)**

Each coordinate is the [arithmetic mean](../../05_Statistics/mean_vs_average/README.md) of the two: the halfway x is the average of the two x's, and the halfway y the average of the two y's. On a number line the point halfway between 2 and 8 is (2 + 8)/2 = 5, and the plane just does that twice, once along each axis. Moving halfway across and halfway up lands you halfway along the diagonal, because the little right triangle from P₁ to M is the big one from P₁ to P₂ at half the size: they are [similar triangles](../../10_Geometry/congruent_and_similar_triangles/README.md) with scale factor ½.

This page follows the second objective of section 1.1, *The Distance and Midpoint Formulas*, in Michael Sullivan's *Precalculus*.

## Symbol by symbol

| Piece | Say it | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| segment P₁P₂ | "segment P one P two" | the straight piece between the two points, ends included. Not the whole line through them. |
| M | "M" | the midpoint, a point: it has two coordinates. |
| (x₁ + x₂)/2 | "x one plus x two, over two" | the average of the x's. A *sum* over 2, not a difference. |
| P₁ + t(P₂ − P₁) | "P one plus t times the trip" | the point a fraction t of the way from P₁ to P₂; t = ½ is the midpoint. |

## Halfway is two claims

The program checks both with the [distance formula](../distance_formula/README.md): M is as far from P₁ as from P₂, and M lies on the segment, because the two half-distances add up to the whole. The second claim is not decoration. Every point on a certain line, the **perpendicular bisector** of the segment, is as far from P₁ as from P₂; section 3 finds two of them with whole-number coordinates. Only one point of that line is on the segment, and it is the midpoint.

## The two traps

- **Half the difference is not the midpoint.** ((x₂ − x₁)/2, (y₂ − y₁)/2) is half of the *trip*, how far to move, not *where* you end up. Added to P₁ it gives the midpoint; on its own it is a different point. Sums go in the midpoint formula; differences go in the distance and slope formulas.
- **Working backwards, do not average again.** Given one end P₁ and the midpoint M, the other end is P₂ = (2·x_M − x₁, 2·y_M − y₁): the far end is as far past M as P₁ is short of it.

## Questions

**1. Find the midpoint of (−5, 5) and (3, 1).**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

((−5 + 3)/2, (5 + 1)/2) = (−1, 3).

</details>

**2. Find the midpoint of (1.5, −2) and (−3.5, 4).**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

((1.5 − 3.5)/2, (−2 + 4)/2) = (−1, 1).

</details>

**3. M = (4, −1) is the midpoint of a segment with one end at (1, 2). Find the other end.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

x₂ = 2 · 4 − 1 = 7 and y₂ = 2 · (−1) − 2 = −4, so (7, −4). Check: ((1 + 7)/2, (2 − 4)/2) = (4, −1).

</details>

**4. Someone writes the midpoint of (2, 3) and (10, 7) as (4, 2). What went wrong, and what is (4, 2)?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

They halved the differences: (10 − 2)/2 = 4 and (7 − 3)/2 = 2. That is half the trip, not a point on the segment. The midpoint is (6, 5) = (2, 3) + (4, 2).

</details>

**5. A point Q is the same distance from A and from B. Is Q the midpoint of AB?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Not necessarily. Every point on the perpendicular bisector of AB is equidistant from A and B. Q is the midpoint only if it also lies on the segment.

</details>

**6. Does the midpoint depend on which point is called P₁?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No: x₁ + x₂ = x₂ + x₁. Addition does not care about order, which is why the midpoint formula has no sign traps at all.

</details>

**7. Show that the midpoint is exactly half the distance from each end, using the distance formula.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

From P₁ to M the x-difference is (x₁ + x₂)/2 − x₁ = (x₂ − x₁)/2, and likewise for y. So each leg is half the full leg, each square a quarter, and d(P₁, M) = √(¼ · d(P₁, P₂)²) = ½ d(P₁, P₂).

</details>

**8. Find the points that divide the segment from (0, 0) to (8, 4) into four equal parts.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

t = ¼, ½, ¾ of the way: (2, 1), (4, 2), (6, 3). The midpoint is the one at t = ½, and it is also the midpoint of the other two.

</details>

**9. The diagonals of the quadrilateral with corners (0, 0), (4, 0), (5, 3), (1, 3) cross. Show that they cut each other in half.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The diagonals are (0, 0)–(5, 3) and (4, 0)–(1, 3). Their midpoints are (2.5, 1.5) and (2.5, 1.5): the same point, so each diagonal passes through the other's midpoint. That is the test for a parallelogram.

</details>

**10. The midpoint is an average. What is the "average" of three points, and what is it for a triangle?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

((x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3, (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3), the centroid, the balancing point of a triangle cut from card. The midpoint is the two-point case.

</details>

## How to practise

1. **Say "sum over two" before writing.** The whole formula is those three words, and saying them keeps the difference out.
2. **Check by distance.** For each answer, compute d(P₁, M)² and d(M, P₂)²: they must be equal and each a quarter of d(P₁, P₂)².
3. **Do one backwards problem for every forwards one.**
4. **The cards**, `trap` tag first.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Środek odcinka to średnia arytmetyczna współrzędnych: M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2). Połowa drogi w poziomie i połowa w pionie dają połowę drogi po przekątnej, bo mały trójkąt od P₁ do M jest podobny do dużego w skali ½.

„W połowie drogi" to dwa warunki: jednakowa odległość od obu końców i leżenie na odcinku. Sam pierwszy warunek spełnia cała prosta, symetralna odcinka. Pułapki: połowa różnicy, ((x₂ − x₁)/2, (y₂ − y₁)/2), to nie środek, tylko połowa przesunięcia; a gdy znamy jeden koniec i środek, drugi koniec to 2M − P₁, nie kolejna średnia. Program sprawdza wszystko wzorem na odległość, na dokładnych ułamkach.
