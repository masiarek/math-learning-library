# Circles: standard form and general form

**Level:** 101 · for anyone starting precalculus

**One line:** A circle is every point at one distance from one point, so its equation is the distance formula held fixed, (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²; the general form hides the center and radius, completing the square brings them back, and when the number that should be r² comes out zero or negative there is no circle at all.

## The idea

A **circle** is the set of all points in the plane at a fixed distance r, the **radius**, from a fixed point (h, k), the **center**. Read with the [distance formula](../distance_formula/README.md), a point (x, y) is on the circle exactly when √((x − h)² + (y − k)²) = r. Square both sides, which is safe because both are non-negative, and that is the equation:

> **(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²**, the **standard form**.

The center and radius are right there to read off, with one trap: the signs. (x + 2)² is (x − (−2))², so its center has x = −2. The **unit circle**, center (0, 0) and radius 1, is x² + y² = 1, and it is where trigonometry will live.

Multiply out the squares and gather everything on one side, and the equation becomes **x² + y² + ax + by + c = 0**, the **general form**. It is what a circle looks like when it turns up in a problem, and the center and radius are no longer visible. To get them back, **complete the square** in x and in y. And if what comes out on the right is 0, the "circle" is one point; if it is negative, there are no points at all, since two squares cannot add up to less than 0.

This page follows section 1.4, *Circles*, in Michael Sullivan's *Precalculus*, the last section of the book's first chapter.

## Symbol by symbol

| Piece | Say it | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| (h, k) | "h, k" | the center. The letters are a convention; they are just the center's x and y. |
| r | "r" | the radius, a length, so r > 0. The equation has r², so read the radius as the square root of the right side. |
| (x − h)² | | the squared horizontal distance from the center. |
| standard form | | (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r², center and radius visible. |
| general form | | x² + y² + ax + by + c = 0, multiplied out. x² and y² always have the *same* coefficient in a circle. |
| complete the square | | x² + ax = (x + a/2)² − (a/2)²: add (a/2)², half the coefficient squared, to make a perfect square, and add the same to the other side. |

## Completing the square, step by step

Take x² + y² + 4x − 6y + 12 = 0.

1. **Group** the x's and the y's, move the number across: (x² + 4x) + (y² − 6y) = −12.
2. **Complete each square**: half of 4 is 2, squared 4; half of −6 is −3, squared 9. Add both **to both sides**: (x² + 4x + 4) + (y² − 6y + 9) = −12 + 4 + 9.
3. **Factor**: (x + 2)² + (y − 3)² = 1.

Center (−2, 3), radius 1. Change the 12 to 13 and the right side becomes 0: only the point (−2, 3). Change it to 14 and the right side is −1: no points. The program runs all three.

## What the program prints

The picture marks every whole-number point exactly 5 from the origin. There are twelve, and each is the corner of a 3-4-5 (or 0-5-5) right triangle, which is the [Pythagorean theorem](../../10_Geometry/pythagorean_theorem/README.md) again. Inside, on, or outside is decided by comparing the squared distance with r², with no square roots.

## Questions

**1. Write the standard form of the circle with center (−2, 3) and radius 4. Then the general form.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(x + 2)² + (y − 3)² = 16. Expanded: x² + 4x + 4 + y² − 6y + 9 = 16, so x² + y² + 4x − 6y − 3 = 0.

</details>

**2. Find the center and radius of (x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 7.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Center (3, −1), radius √7. Not 7: the right side is r².

</details>

**3. Find the center and radius of x² + y² − 4x + 6y − 3 = 0.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(x² − 4x + 4) + (y² + 6y + 9) = 3 + 4 + 9 = 16, so (x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 16: center (2, −3), radius 4.

</details>

**4. Find the center and radius of 2x² + 2y² − 12x + 8y − 24 = 0.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Divide by 2 first: x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0. Then (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 12 + 9 + 4 = 25: center (3, −2), radius 5. Completing the square only works with x² and y² having coefficient 1.

</details>

**5. Is x² + y² + 4x − 6y + 14 = 0 a circle?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No. Completing the square gives (x + 2)² + (y − 3)² = −1, and a sum of squares is never negative. The graph is empty.

</details>

**6. Is x² + 2y² = 4 a circle?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No: x² and y² have different coefficients. It is an ellipse, stretched along the x-axis, with intercepts ±2 and ±√2.

</details>

**7. Find the intercepts of (x − 1)² + (y + 2)² = 5.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

y = 0: (x − 1)² + 4 = 5, x − 1 = ±1, x = 0 or 2. x = 0: 1 + (y + 2)² = 5, y + 2 = ±2, y = 0 or −4. 0 is in both lists: the circle passes through the origin.

</details>

**8. Find the equation of the circle with a diameter from (1, 4) to (−3, 2).**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The center is the [midpoint](../midpoint_formula/README.md), (−1, 3). The radius is the distance from the center to an end: √(4 + 1) = √5. So (x + 1)² + (y − 3)² = 5.

</details>

**9. The circle has center (2, −3) and passes through (5, 1). Find its equation.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

r² = (5 − 2)² + (1 + 3)² = 9 + 16 = 25. (x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 25. No square root needed, because the equation wants r².

</details>

**10. Is (0, 0) inside, on or outside the circle (x + 2)² + (y − 3)² = 16?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(0 + 2)² + (0 − 3)² = 13 < 16: inside.

</details>

**11. Which symmetries does every circle centered at the origin have? Every circle centered at (2, −3)?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

x² + y² = r² passes all three tests of [graphs, intercepts and symmetry](../graphs_intercepts_symmetry/README.md): x-axis, y-axis and origin. A circle centered at (2, −3) is symmetric about the vertical line x = 2, the horizontal line y = −3, and its center, but in general about none of the axes.

</details>

**12. Why can (3/5, 4/5) be written down as a point of the unit circle without a calculator?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(3/5)² + (4/5)² = 9/25 + 16/25 = 1. It is the 3-4-5 triangle scaled by 1/5. Every Pythagorean triple a, b, c gives the point (a/c, b/c).

</details>

## How to practise

1. **Read center and radius aloud with the sign flip.** (x + 2)² + (y − 3)² = 7: "center minus two, three; radius root seven."
2. **Completing the square, all three steps written out.** Group, add half-squared to both sides, factor. Check the result by expanding it again.
3. **Divide first** when x² and y² carry a coefficient.
4. **Check with the program**, and do the cards.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Okrąg to zbiór punktów odległych o r od środka (h, k). Wzór na odległość, podniesiony do kwadratu, daje od razu równanie w postaci kanonicznej: (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². Pułapka ze znakami: (x + 2)² znaczy, że środek ma x = −2; a po prawej stronie jest r², więc promień to pierwiastek z tej liczby.

Po wymnożeniu dostajemy postać ogólną x² + y² + ax + by + c = 0, w której środka i promienia nie widać. Odzyskuje się je, uzupełniając do kwadratu: do x² + ax dodajemy (a/2)², do obu stron równania. Jeśli po prawej wyjdzie liczba dodatnia, to okrąg; zero, to jeden punkt; ujemna, to nic, bo suma kwadratów nie jest ujemna. Gdy przy x² i y² stoi ta sama liczba różna od 1, najpierw przez nią dzielimy; gdy różne liczby, to nie okrąg, tylko elipsa. Program rozstrzyga to dokładnie dla dowolnych współczynników.
