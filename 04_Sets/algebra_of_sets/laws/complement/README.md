# The complement laws: A ∪ A′ = U and A ∩ A′ = ∅

**Level:** 101 · one law of [the algebra of sets](../../README.md), on its own page

**One line:** Every member of the universe is in A or out of it, and none is both: the law of the excluded middle and the law of non-contradiction, written for sets.

## The law

| Sets | Logic | Python |
|---|---|---|
| A ∪ A′ = U, A ∩ A′ = ∅ | p ∨ ¬p = true, p ∧ ¬p = false | `a | (U - a) == U and a & (U - a) == set()` |

## Why it is true

For each x in U, "x ∈ A or x ∉ A" is true, since one of the two must hold, and "x ∈ A and x ∉ A" is false, since both cannot. So the union of A with its complement is all of U and the intersection is empty. The complement needs U fixed in advance: A′ means U ∖ A, and [the parent page](../../README.md#the-complement-needs-a-universe) says why. A law of sets is decided one member at a time, so checking it on every subset of a four-element universe, every pattern of membership there is, proves it; the program does that and prints the matching truth table.

## Where it is used, and the trap

This is the law behind every case split: to prove something for all x, prove it for x ∈ A and for x ∉ A, and the two cases cover everything and overlap nowhere. It is also the one law a fuzzy or three-valued logic gives up, as [day or night ↗](https://masiarek.github.io/learning-to-learn-library/03_Believing_You_Can/day_or_night/) shows: twilight is in "day" and in "not day" a little each.
