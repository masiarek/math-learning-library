# The double complement law: (A′)′ = A

**Level:** 101 · one law of [the algebra of sets](../../README.md), on its own page

**One line:** Not not in A is in A: two complements cancel, so a chain of primes can be cut to its parity.

## The law

| Sets | Logic | Python |
|---|---|---|
| (A′)′ = A | ¬¬p = p | `U - (U - a) == a` |

## Why it is true

x ∈ (A′)′ means x ∉ A′, which means it is not the case that x ∉ A, which means x ∈ A. Each step is one use of the definition of the complement, and "not not p" is p in classical logic. A law of sets is decided one member at a time, so checking it on every subset of a four-element universe, every pattern of membership there is, proves it; the program does that and prints the matching truth table.

## Where it is used, and the trap

In code, `not not x` is `bool(x)`, and two negations in a condition are a sign to rewrite it. In [De Morgan's laws](../de_morgan/README.md) the double complement is what lets you go back: from (A′ ∩ B′)′ to A ∪ B. The one logic that refuses this law is intuitionistic logic, where "not not p" is weaker than p, because a proof that p cannot be refuted is not a proof of p.
