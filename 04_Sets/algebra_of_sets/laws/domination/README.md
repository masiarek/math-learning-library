# The domination laws: A ∪ U = U and A ∩ ∅ = ∅

**Level:** 101 · one law of [the algebra of sets](../../README.md), on its own page

**One line:** The universe swallows any union and the empty set swallows any intersection: once U or ∅ is in the expression, A no longer matters.

## The law

| Sets | Logic | Python |
|---|---|---|
| A ∪ U = U, A ∩ ∅ = ∅ | p ∨ true = true, p ∧ false = false | `a | U == U and a & set() == set()` |

## Why it is true

"x ∈ A or x ∈ U" is true for every x of the universe whatever A is, so the union is U; "x ∈ A and x ∈ ∅" is false for every x, so the intersection is ∅. In logic, *or true* is true and *and false* is false, whatever p says. A law of sets is decided one member at a time, so checking it on every subset of a four-element universe, every pattern of membership there is, proves it; the program does that and prints the matching truth table.

## Where it is used, and the trap

These are the short-circuit rules of every programming language: `a or True` is True and `a and False` is False without looking at a, which is exactly why `x in A or x in U` can skip the first test. The trap is reading them as saying something about A; they say A is irrelevant.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Prawa pochłaniania przez U i ∅: uniwersum pochłania każdą sumę, zbiór pusty każdy przekrój, więc A przestaje mieć znaczenie. W kodzie to „leniwe" `or True` i `and False`.

## Auf Deutsch: Stichwörter

Dominanzgesetze: A ∪ U = U und A ∩ ∅ = ∅.

**Stichwörter:** Dominanzgesetz (absorbierendes Element), Grundmenge, leere Menge.
