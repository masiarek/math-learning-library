# The distributive laws: each of ∪ and ∩ distributes over the other

**Level:** 101 · one law of [the algebra of sets](../../README.md), on its own page

**One line:** Unlike numbers, where × distributes over + and not the other way, each of ∪ and ∩ distributes over the other, so set algebra is more symmetric than arithmetic.

## The law

| Sets | Logic | Python |
|---|---|---|
| A ∩ (B ∪ C) = (A ∩ B) ∪ (A ∩ C), A ∪ (B ∩ C) = (A ∪ B) ∩ (A ∪ C) | p ∧ (q ∨ r) = (p ∧ q) ∨ (p ∧ r), p ∨ (q ∧ r) = (p ∨ q) ∧ (p ∨ r) | `a & (b | c) == (a & b) | (a & c)` |

## Why it is true

"x ∈ A and (x ∈ B or x ∈ C)" means "(x ∈ A and x ∈ B) or (x ∈ A and x ∈ C)": the *and* is applied to whichever of the two held. The second law is the first with the roles swapped, and it holds too, which is where the analogy with + and × breaks: a + (b × c) ≠ (a + b) × (a + c). A law of sets is decided one member at a time, so checking it on every subset of a four-element universe, every pattern of membership there is, proves it; the program does that and prints the matching truth table.

## Where it is used, and the trap

Distributivity is how a condition is put into normal form before a database or a SAT solver sees it, and how `WHERE a AND (b OR c)` becomes two simpler queries joined by `OR`. The second law is the surprising one; it is why a [Boolean algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_algebra_(structure)) is not a ring in disguise, and why a Venn diagram, which [the parent page](../../README.md#venn-diagrams) explains, can prove both at once by shading.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Prawa rozdzielności: każde z działań ∪ i ∩ jest rozdzielne względem drugiego, inaczej niż u liczb, gdzie × jest rozdzielne względem +, ale nie odwrotnie. Dowód: „x ∈ A i (x ∈ B lub x ∈ C)" to „(x ∈ A i x ∈ B) lub (x ∈ A i x ∈ C)".

## Auf Deutsch: Stichwörter

Distributivgesetze: ∪ und ∩ distribuieren jedes über das andere, anders als + und · bei Zahlen.

**Stichwörter:** Distributivgesetz, Vereinigung, Schnitt, Vergleich mit Addition und Multiplikation.
