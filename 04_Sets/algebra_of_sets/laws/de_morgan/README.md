# De Morgan's laws: (A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∩ B′ and (A ∩ B)′ = A′ ∪ B′

**Level:** 101 · one law of [the algebra of sets](../../README.md), on its own page

**One line:** Taking the complement swaps ∪ and ∩: *not either* is *neither*, and *not both* is *at least one missing*; it is the one law worth memorising in words, because it is the one people get wrong in code.

## The law

| Sets | Logic | Python |
|---|---|---|
| (A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∩ B′, (A ∩ B)′ = A′ ∪ B′ | ¬(p ∨ q) = ¬p ∧ ¬q, ¬(p ∧ q) = ¬p ∨ ¬q | `U - (a | b) == (U - a) & (U - b)` |

## Why it is true

x ∉ A ∪ B means x is in neither, so x ∉ A and x ∉ B; x ∉ A ∩ B means x fails to be in both, so x ∉ A or x ∉ B. Pushing a *not* through an *or* turns it into an *and*, and through an *and* into an *or*. A law of sets is decided one member at a time, so checking it on every subset of a four-element universe, every pattern of membership there is, proves it; the program does that and prints the matching truth table.

## Where it is used, and the trap

`not (a or b)` is `not a and not b`; in SQL, `NOT (x = 1 OR y = 2)` is `x <> 1 AND y <> 2`. The classic bug is "not both" written as `not (a or b)` when it should be `not (a and b)`, that is `not a or not b`. The same law for quantifiers, ¬∀ is ∃¬, is on [predicates and quantifiers](../../../../11_Logic/predicates_and_quantifiers/README.md), and the version for infinitely many sets is Hrbacek and Jech's exercise 3.11 on [the function katas](../../../function_katas/README.md) page. Augustus De Morgan stated the laws in 1847; the idea is in William of Ockham.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Prawa De Morgana: dopełnienie zamienia sumę z przekrojem; „nie (któryś)" to „żaden", a „nie (oba)" to „co najmniej jednego brak". To prawo warto umieć na pamięć słowami, bo właśnie je psuje się w kodzie: `not (a or b)` to `not a and not b`.

## Auf Deutsch: Stichwörter

De-Morgan-Gesetze: das Komplement einer Vereinigung ist der Schnitt der Komplemente, und umgekehrt.

**Stichwörter:** De-Morgan-Gesetze, Komplement, Negation von „oder“ und „und“, Dualität.
