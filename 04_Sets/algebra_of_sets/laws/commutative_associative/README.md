# The commutative and associative laws: order and grouping do not matter

**Level:** 101 · one law of [the algebra of sets](../../README.md), on its own page

**One line:** A ∪ B ∪ C needs no brackets and no order, because "x is in at least one of them" does not care which was named first or paired first; the same for "x is in all of them".

## The law

| Sets | Logic | Python |
|---|---|---|
| A ∪ B = B ∪ A, A ∩ B = B ∩ A; (A ∪ B) ∪ C = A ∪ (B ∪ C), (A ∩ B) ∩ C = A ∩ (B ∩ C) | p ∨ q = q ∨ p, (p ∨ q) ∨ r = p ∨ (q ∨ r), and the same for ∧ | `a | b == b | a and (a | b) | c == a | (b | c)` |

## Why it is true

"x ∈ A or x ∈ B" and "x ∈ B or x ∈ A" are the same claim, and "x is in A or in (B or C)" is "x is in at least one of A, B, C" however the brackets fall. *Or* and *and* are commutative and associative in logic, so ∪ and ∩ are for sets. A law of sets is decided one member at a time, so checking it on every subset of a four-element universe, every pattern of membership there is, proves it; the program does that and prints the matching truth table.

## Where it is used, and the trap

Associativity is what lets Python write `set().union(a, b, c)` and `a | b | c` without brackets, and what makes ∪ of a whole family, [the axiom of unions](../../../../13_Axioms_of_Set_Theory/unions/README.md), well defined. The trap is set difference: A ∖ B ≠ B ∖ A and (A ∖ B) ∖ C ≠ A ∖ (B ∖ C), which [the parent page](../../README.md#laws-that-look-true-and-are-not) finds by search, so ∖ must be bracketed always.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Prawa przemienności i łączności: w sumie i przekroju kolejność ani nawiasy nie mają znaczenia, bo „x jest w co najmniej jednym" i „x jest we wszystkich" nie zależą od kolejności. Różnica zbiorów nie ma żadnej z tych własności i zawsze wymaga nawiasów.
