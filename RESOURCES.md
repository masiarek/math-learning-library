# Resources

**Level:** reference · for finding the books and talks this library leans on

**One line:** The outside sources behind the pages, grouped by what they are for, each with the page that uses it.

A page here cites its sources in its own **See also**; this page gathers the main ones in one place. It is not a reading list for mathematics in general: every entry is here because a page uses it.

## Learning how to learn

- **Saundra Yancy McGuire with Stephanie McGuire, *Teach Yourself How to Learn: Strategies You Can Use to Ace Any Course at Any Level*** (Stylus, 2018), foreword by Mark McDaniel. The book behind [12_Learning_to_Learn](12_Learning_to_Learn/README.md): metacognition and Flavell's four abilities (chapter 3, Figure 3.2), the Count the Vowels exercise (Figure 3.3), studying versus learning, Bloom's taxonomy and the study cycle with intense study sessions (chapter 4). Short, practical, and written for students; start here.
- **Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III and Mark A. McDaniel, *Make It Stick: The Science of Successful Learning*** (Harvard University Press, 2014). The research on retrieval practice, spacing and interleaving, told for a general reader. McGuire recommends it as a place to start on the science; used in [spaced retrieval](12_Learning_to_Learn/spaced_retrieval/README.md).
- **John Medina, *Brain Rules*** (Pear Press, 2008). McGuire's other recommendation on the cognitive science of memory, cited for reviewing soon after class.
- **Terry Doyle and Todd Zakrajsek, *The New Science of Learning: How to Learn in Harmony With Your Brain*** (Stylus, 2013). Cited by McGuire for breaks, rest and naps after learning.
- **Carol S. Dweck, *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success*** (Random House, 2006). Fixed and growth mindsets: whether you believe ability can be developed changes how you meet challenges, setbacks, effort and criticism. McGuire's chapter 6 builds on it, including a section on stereotypes about mathematical ability. Later large studies found the effect of mindset interventions on grades real but small, so it is best read as one ingredient, not the whole recipe.
- **Susan A. Ambrose and others, *How Learning Works: Seven Research-Based Principles for Smart Teaching*** (Jossey-Bass, 2010). Written for teachers, useful to learners. McGuire's chapter 7 takes from it the three levers of motivation: the **value** of the goal, whether the **environment** feels supportive, and **belief in the ability to succeed**. Its chapter on how students organise knowledge is the research behind [count the vowels](12_Learning_to_Learn/count_the_vowels/README.md).
- **Lorin W. Anderson and David R. Krathwohl (eds.), *A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing*** (Longman, 2001). The revised Bloom's taxonomy, used in [studying vs learning](12_Learning_to_Learn/studying_vs_learning/README.md).
- **Henry L. Roediger III and Jeffrey D. Karpicke, "Test-enhanced learning"**, *Psychological Science* 17 (2006). The experiment in which rereaders felt surer and remembered less; used in [metacognition](12_Learning_to_Learn/metacognition/README.md).
- **Barbara Oakley, *A Mind for Numbers: How to Excel at Math and Science (Even If You Flunked Algebra)*** (TarcherPerigee, 2014). Focused and diffuse thinking, and why stepping away from a stuck problem helps: the break in McGuire's intense study session, explained. Written for exactly the reader of this library.
- **[Learning How to Learn ↗](https://www.coursera.org/learn/learning-how-to-learn)**, a free Coursera course by Barbara Oakley and Terrence Sejnowski: the same ideas as *A Mind for Numbers*, in short videos, with a section on procrastination.
- **Scott H. Young, *Ultralearning*** (Harper Business, 2019). Self-directed, project-based learning of hard skills, with retrieval and feedback at its centre. Useful for working through a whole book or subject, such as a chapter here, on your own.
- **Huberman Lab podcast**, Andrew Huberman's episodes on focus and on neuroplasticity: the biology of attention and learning, told at length for a general audience. Background rather than method.
- **[Anki ↗](https://apps.ankiweb.net/)**, the free flashcard program that reads the decks on this library's pages, and **[SuperMemo's SM-2 ↗](https://super-memory.com/english/ol/sm2.htm)**, the scheduling algorithm it started from.
